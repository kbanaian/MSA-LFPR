Labor force participation and employment: demographic detail

A single-metro rebuild of the econvitals national demographic-detail page. It keeps the same three experiments: actual vs. age/sex-adjusted rates, an aging-only counterfactual from a chosen base year, and a by-group table. The data underneath had to change, because the CPS can't support a single metro. The plumbing is laid out at the bottom of the page.

Actual vs. age/sex-adjusted

Each year's change is split into a behavior part (rates within sex × age cells moving) and a composition part (the population mix shifting). Only the behavior part is accumulated, then anchored to the latest actual rate. The gap between the lines is how much the changing age/sex mix has moved the headline since the first year.

Monthly read between ACS releases

ACS arrives about nine months after the year ends. For timeliness, the page divides BLS LAUS labor force and employment (not seasonally adjusted) by ACS civilian population 16+, interpolated by month and extended past the last ACS year at its trailing five-year growth rate. LAUS and ACS levels need not agree (different survey controls; the ACS denominator includes the institutional population), so read the LAUS line for direction, not level. The extrapolated denominator also means the most recent months partly reflect a population assumption.

Demographic contributions to the change

Each line is a group's cumulative behavior contribution (percentage points) to the change since the first year, chained year to year. The national page shows 5-year trailing averages of monthly data; with annual ACS points, the cumulative path is easier to read.

Aging-only counterfactual, since a chosen base year

The opposite experiment: hold every sex × age cell's rate at its base-year value and let only the population mix evolve. The national page offers the 2000–01, 2007 and 2019–20 peaks. Here, the bases are the first ACS year in the data and whichever configured business-cycle peaks it covers.

Beyond age and sex: nativity and school enrollment

The age/sex decomposition counts everything except aging as "behavior," including a changing mix of immigrants and students. ACS microdata (PUMS) lets those two dimensions be added to the cells. Each window is a 5-year PUMS file, and the error bars are 90% margins of error computed from the 80 replicate weights, the Census Bureau's own method.
